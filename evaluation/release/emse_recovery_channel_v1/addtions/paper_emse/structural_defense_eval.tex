\section{RQ4: Structural Defense as a Design Implication}
\label{sec:structural-eval}

RQ4 is retained as a supplementary design implication rather than a co-equal
empirical storyline.  The current evidence freeze contains no independently
auditable native-framework experiment for \reauthgate{}; consequently, this
section does not report the earlier author-constructed point estimates or
latency numbers as empirical results.  They should not be pooled with the
Reflexion, AutoGen, or Aider recovery-path measurements.

\paragraph{Design claim.}
\reauthgate{} applies complete mediation~\cite{saltzer1975protection} at the
recovery sink.  A recovery action that changes roles, tools, or topology is
represented as a deferred side effect and must pass the same authorization
check that guarded the failed forward action.  The intended invariant is
\[
  P(\text{recovery action}) \leq P(\text{failed execution}),
\]
where $P$ is the framework's authorization order.  A whitelist or policy
check can enforce this invariant independently of the LLM's response.

\paragraph{Why it complements TrustOrigin.}
\trustorigin{} addresses the content-level channel: it preserves source
provenance and downgrades externally originated failure content before the
next LLM turn.  That control is probabilistic because the model may still
follow an injected instruction.  \reauthgate{} addresses a different sink:
even if the model proposes a privileged action, the orchestrator rejects it
unless the authorization invariant holds.  The two controls therefore defend
content trust and action authority respectively; neither should be presented
as a substitute for the other.

\paragraph{Verification requirement.}
Before RQ4 is promoted beyond a design implication, the replication package
must include a fixed framework commit, an executable native recovery path,
matched attack and benign workloads, raw per-trial logs, and an independent
check that every side effect crosses the gate.  A future multi-framework
port (two or three frameworks) should report the deterministic by-construction
property separately from probabilistic ASR estimates.  Until that evidence is
available, claims about zero ASR, framework-agnosticity, or negligible
overhead remain implementation hypotheses rather than findings of this paper.

Summary

Multi-tenant agent runtimes multiplex tenant work, permission checks, and audit
activity over finite executors. We show that queueing and lock occupancy can
delay a permission decision beyond its deadline; when the fallback is
permissive, executor unavailability becomes an unsafe admission. Neuron
reserves permission capacity and passes a measured resource-pressure signal to
the escalation policy. A Java 21 evaluation compares shared fail-open,
shared fail-closed, isolated, and coupled architectures across two recorded
environments, deadline and attacker-pressure surfaces, capacity sweeps, mixed
load QoS, fixed API-trace replay, a process-separated permission service, and
an additional executor-focused replication. The results separate the two
mechanisms: capacity reservation protects timely decisions, while coupling
changes a depth-1 verdict under pressure. The artifact releases raw JSONL,
environment records, scripts, generated manuscript values, and reproduction
instructions. The process-separated service is a same-host loopback study and
does not establish WAN or multi-node behavior.
